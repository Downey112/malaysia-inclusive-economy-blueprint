Core Proposal Action Pillars

The primary structural interventions designed to address wealth concentration and low-wage precarity.

01

Progressive Direct Taxation

Implement Inheritance Duty on multi-million estates, Capital Gains Tax on shares, and tax elite public pensions to replace regressive consumption taxes (GST).

02

Livable Wages & Fair Hiring

Mandate annual minimum wage increases toward a RM3,000 floor, paired with mandatory ethnic-blind resume screening in entry-level hiring.

03

PPR "Cash Plus" Safety Net

Combine direct cash transfers with mandatory maternal healthcare and child nutrition vouchers for low-income PPR public housing families.

04

13MP Macro Targets

Replace symbolic poverty drop goals with ambitious OECD-aligned equality benchmarks (Gini 0.320) and state-funded housing construction.

Distributional National Accounts (DINA) & Wealth Disparity

Research by Khalid & Yang (2019/2020) reveals that standard household income surveys understate economic inequality by omitting capital gains, undistributed corporate profits, and offshore asset holdings.

Data Source: Khalid & Yang (2019/2020) Distributional National Accounts for Malaysia.

Top 1% Wealth Share 32.4%

The richest 1% holds nearly one-third of all total private national wealth in Malaysia, while bearing negligible liability under consumption tax regimes.

Bottom 50% Wealth Share 3.2%

The bottom half of Malaysian society holds less than 3.5% of total wealth, leaving them vulnerable to inflation and regressive consumption taxes like GST.

Key Policy Implication:

Taxing labor and consumption while leaving capital stock untaxed expands structural wealth inequality.
